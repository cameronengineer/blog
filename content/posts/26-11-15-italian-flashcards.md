I've been making Anki flashcards for learning Italian, and I've put them on a [small website](https://italianflashcards.cameronengineer.com/) so other people can download them too.

The collection covers everyday vocabulary, words grouped by CILS level, numbers and verb forms. There are also decks built from the subtitles of *Le otto montagne* and *Benvenuti al Sud*, with the word shown in a line of dialogue and a word-for-word English translation.

I wanted more than a word on one side and a translation on the other. The cards include pictures and recordings, with details such as gender and plurals where relevant. Some have a short note about the word's origin or a connection to English. The website shows the front and back of a real card so you can see what you're downloading.

The number deck pays particular attention to numbers I mix up, especially the teens and tens. Verb practice is split into separate tense decks, so I can work on one tense at a time.

I used AI to write the cards and generate the pictures and voices, with dictionary data from Wiktionary as a reference. That made building the collection practical, but it also means there will be mistakes. They're the cards I'm using for my own study, and I'll keep improving them as I go.

The decks are free to download as `.apkg` files, with pictures and recordings included. Import one into Anki to use it. The vocabulary decks overlap, particularly the level decks, so you probably don't need them all.
